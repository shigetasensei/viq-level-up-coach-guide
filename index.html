The 5-minute briefing

If you read nothing else, read this. Each point links to the part of the guide that goes deeper.

  1. Move Bean Bags into Goals

    That's the whole objective. Higher Goals score more: 1, 3, 6, 12 and 16 points per bag. Scoring →

  2. Color matters

    Red and blue bags only score in their own color's Goals. Yellow scores anywhere, and only yellow scores on L4. Colors →

  3. One bag at a time

    A robot may Possess or Plow only one Bean Bag at a time. This shapes every design and strategy decision. Match →

  4. Two robots, one score

    Teamwork Matches pair two teams for 60 seconds. Both teams receive the same Alliance score. Events →

  5. Students lead, you guide

    Students design, build, program and strategize. Adults can't be in the Driver Station or direct play during a Match. Coaching →

First season?

New coaches & rookie teams

You don't need to be an engineer or programmer. VEX's own coach guidance says the coach's main job is to create an environment where students ask questions and own their work. This section turns the season into steps you can tick off.

Your season roadmap

  1. Set up the team. A Team is three or more students, with at least one responsible adult (that's you). Register the team for the season on events.vex.com (new team or renewal), and complete the coach background check the VEX Library describes. Every coach needs one before the team can register for an event.
  2. Get the gear. A VEX IQ Competition Kit (2nd generation) for the robot, plus the Field and Game Elements for Level Up. A practice field is a big help, and Resources links build instructions.
  3. Set expectations with families. Send the Letter Home to Parents, and read the Youth Protection Policy, Student-Centered Policy and Code of Conduct before you start. Collect the participant release forms each student needs (S3).
  4. Book your first event early. VEX suggests choosing by cost, travel, event type and your team's goals, and competing early and often. A first event is a learning event, not a verdict on the season.
  5. Run your first sessions. Team rules and goals, the game reveal, practice with Virtual Driving Skills, then building a first robot (see the plan below).
  6. Start the engineering notebook on day one. Students write it. Coaches may teach notebook skills but cannot write in it.
  7. Pre-inspect and rehearse. Run a mock Match with a timer, a driver hand-off and a loader, and walk the inspection checklist.
  8. Compete, then debrief. Capture lessons in the notebook and decide what to improve for event two.

The engineering notebook, simply

  • Students write it. Coaches and mentors can help students build notebook skills but cannot write in it. Paper or digital both work.
  • Three one-page summaries go with it for judging: a Season Summary, a Code Summary and a Credit Summary.
  • Start with the student-friendly rubric. VEX suggests beginning with the “Is our notebook easy to use?” section.
  • Tip: five minutes at the end of each meeting to record what was tried, what happened and what's next beats a big catch-up before the event.

What a first event looks like

  1. Check-in and inspection. The robot must pass before any Match (R2).
  2. Qualification Matches. You'll play at least six, with a randomly assigned partner each time (T9).
  3. Robot Skills. Three Driving and three Autonomous Coding runs, first come first served (T15).
  4. Finals and awards. The top-ranked teams pair up for Finals Matches (T14), then awards.
  5. Plan the day with the schedule planner.

Questions rookie coaches ask

Do the same students have to drive every Match?

No. You can put different students in the Driver 1, Driver 2 and Loader roles from Match to Match, and the manual's glossary says there is no requirement that the same students serve as Drivers in multiple Matches. The rules are: all Drive Team Members must be students on your own team, no student can fill more than one Drive Team role in the same Match, and within a Match the two Drivers must hand over the controller between 0:35 and 0:25 remaining (GG1, GG11). Many teams rotate roles so every student gets field time and drivers don't get tired, but a consistent pair can also build chemistry. Both are legal, so decide as a team and let students choose.

Do I have to read the whole manual?

The manual is long, and VEX's guidance is to treat it as a reference. Start with the Quick Reference Guide at the front, give students the Simplified Edition appendix, then use this site's rules explorer to look things up as questions come up. Everyone who drives should know the one-bag limit, the hand-off, and the Load Zone rules.

What if our robot isn't finished by the event?

Bring it anyway. A robot must be in the Driver Station or on the Field for its Match even if it doesn't work, otherwise it's a no-show. A team with a non-working robot still gets its partner's Alliance score as long as it's present (GG2). Inspection still applies, so keep it legal.

Can I help fix the robot or fix the code?

No. Adults can teach and ask questions but may not do the building, programming, notebook or strategy. Doing so can become a violation as soon as that robot scores (G2). The coaching section has a quick do/don't list.

Our team has only three students. Is that enough?

Yes. A Team is three or more students. In a Match each team uses two Drivers and one Loader, so with three students everyone is on the Drive Team. Each student can fill only one Drive Team role in a given Match.

What if we disagree with a call?

Only the Drive Team Members from that Match may raise it with the Head Referee, right after the Match, from the Driver Station. The Head Referee's decision is final (GG14). Practice the polite version at home.

Does a rookie team need to win to be successful?

No. Rankings are only one part of VEX IQ. Student ownership, teamwork, a well-kept notebook and improvement across events all matter. Ask students at the end of event one: what worked, what broke, and what will we change?

The game

The Field is 6′ × 8′ (six by eight tiles). Higher Goals earn more points, but most Bean Bags have to travel a long way to reach them, and the Field offers longer open routes and shorter, tighter shortcuts.

What's on the Field

Bean Bags38 total: 16 red + 16 blue (8 of each start on the Field, 8 of each are loaded) and 6 yellow (2 are the robots' Preloads, 4 start on the Field)
Goals2 Floor Goals, 2 Pyramid Goals (one red, one blue), 2 L4 Goals
Load Zones2 (one red, one blue) where Loaders add Bean Bags
OtherTwo wall structures and two Floor barriers shape the routes robots can take

A Bean Bag is roughly 3″ × 4″ and about 40 g.

Which bags score where?

Yellow is the flexible, scarce resource: only 6 of the 38 Bean Bags are yellow, and 2 of those are the robots' Preloads. Brief any color-blind students on the Goal labels (L1–L4, red/blue side) and on bag position rather than relying on color alone.

Terms you'll hear constantly

Preload
The one yellow Bean Bag each robot starts the Match touching.
Loader
The third Drive Team Member, who adds Bean Bags to the Field through the Load Zone.
Possession
A Bean Bag is fully supported by the robot, or moves along with the robot however it turns and drives.
Plowing
Intentionally pushing an object across the Floor without possessing it.
Alliance
Two teams paired for a Teamwork Match; both get the same score.
Minor / Major Violation
Minor: no Disqualification. Major: results in a Disqualification. Score-affecting violations are generally Major.
Score Affecting
A violation that improved the Alliance's score by the end of the Match.
Skills Stop Time
Time remaining when a team opts to end a Skills run early; used only as a tiebreaker.

Scoring

Scores are judged visually by the Head Referee after the Match ends, once everything has come to rest. No video review.

What makes a bag count? SC3 · SC4 · SC5
  • Never touching a robot when scoring is judged.
  • Floor Goal: entirely inside the Goal's boundaries. A bag overhanging the border doesn't count.
  • L1–L3: resting on that level's surface, or supported (directly or indirectly) by a bag that is. It must not be touching anything outside the Field.
  • L4: resting on the L4 surface or on a bag that is, and it must be yellow.
  • Stacks: a bag that counts on two levels earns only the higher level's points.
  • Color: must match the Goal, or be yellow (L4: yellow only).

Coaching angle: points per trip

Because a robot can carry only one bag per trip, every cycle is one bag. An L3 bag is worth 12 Floor Goal bags, but the trip is longer and the placement harder. The GDC's own design note says the best strategies balance how many points per bag, how quickly you score, and how reliably your robot can repeat the cycle. That's a great conversation to have with your team's strategist.

    How a Match works

    A Teamwork Match is 60 seconds of driver-controlled play by two robots working as an Alliance. There is no autonomous period in Teamwork Matches.

    Drive Team

    Up to three students per team in the Driver Station: Driver 1, Driver 2 and a Loader. Only they may be there. Adults other than event staff may not.

    GG1 · GG11

    Starting

    Robot within 11″×20″×15″, stationary, touching only the Floor, the Field Perimeter and its yellow Preload. Team 1/red starts beside the blue Pyramid Goal; Team 2/blue beside the red one.

    SG1 · SG5

    Growing mid-Match

    Height is unlimited after the start. Length may grow to 24″, but the robot must never exceed an 11″×24″ footprint, even while swinging a lift out.

    SG2 · SG3

    Loading

    One bag at a time through the matching-color Load Zone, flat on the Floor, fully inside the Zone, and only when the Zone is empty. Never place a bag directly on a robot.

    SG7

    Out of the Field

    Bags that leave the Field aren't coming back, and a bag that falls inside the Pyramid structure counts as out. Resetting a stuck robot permanently removes any bag it was controlling.

    SG4 · GG10

    The buzzer

    Robots and drivers must stop at 0:00. A bag released before 0:00 still counts when it comes to rest. Scoring from motion after 0:00 never counts, and in Teamwork Matches it is also a (Minor) violation.

    GG12

    Common inspection stumbling blocks

    • Uninspected configurations. If the robot can fold, swap attachments or start in several poses, show the inspector each one. You can't be inspected in one and start in another.
    • “Hidden” extras. Extra motors, batteries or Brains count even if unplugged or unused.
    • 3-D printing. Banned for everything, including license plates, holders and decorations.
    • Decorations that do a job. If removing the decoration would change how the robot performs, inspectors may treat it as functional.
    • Firmware. The minimum can change mid-season; you get a 14-day grace period.
    • Parts lists. Use the official Legal and Illegal Parts documents (see Resources); they update independently of the manual.

    Coaching the team

    VEX IQ is a student-centered program. Your job is to teach, mentor and keep the team organized, not to do the work.

    ✓ You can

    • Teach concepts: engineering, programming, strategy, notebooking.
    • Ask questions that help students find their own answers.
    • Give tips (not solutions) and help the team plan time.
    • Cheer positively during a Match.
    • Post rules questions to the official Q&A from the team's events.vex.com account.
    • Be present for the whole event, as the supervising adult.

    ✕ You can't

    • Build, repair, design or program the robot, or write the notebook or strategy.
    • Enter the Driver Station during a Match.
    • Direct, command or advise the Drive Team during a Match.
    • Speak to the Head Referee about a score or ruling. Only the Drive Team Members may.
    • Ask scorekeepers or other staff to explain a ruling.
    • Expect video or photo review. It isn't used.

    Penalty traps worth drilling

    These are the situations where teams most easily collect violations. Each ties to a rule you can read in full in the explorer.

    Second bag in the robot

    Carrying or pushing more than one bag stops the robot cold until the extra is shed.

    SG6

    Late or early hand-off

    The controller must switch between 0:35 and 0:25. The first Driver may not touch controls afterwards.

    GG11

    Hands in the Field

    No reaching in during a Match, except for loading and approved resets.

    GG4

    Loading mistakes

    Wrong-color Zone, bag overlapping the Zone line, loading while another bag touches the Zone, or placing a bag onto a robot.

    SG7

    Scoring after the buzzer

    Motion past 0:00 earns a violation in Teamwork Matches, and any points from it are ignored.

    GG12

    Footprint creep

    A lift that swings out beyond 11″ × 24″ mid-Match breaks the size limit even if it ends within bounds.

    SG2

    No-shows

    A robot that isn't at the Field for its Match scores zero and is never dropped from rankings.

    GG2 · T10

    Arguing a call

    Appeals happen at the Field, right after the Match, by the Drive Team. After the final decision, it's final.

    GG14 · T3

    Practice ideas

    These are suggestions from this guide's author, not part of the manual.

    • One-bag discipline. Run cycles with a referee-minded teammate watching for any second bag being Possessed or Plowed.
    • Hand-off at a fixed point. Pick a repeatable moment (e.g. 0:30) to pass the controller, then rehearse it until it's automatic.
    • Load rhythm. Practice the Loader and Driver together: bag placed, hand clear, robot approaches.
    • Size and swing check. Measure the robot at start and at the worst-case extension of every mechanism.
    • Reset drill. Rehearse a GG10 reset: controller down, wait for permission, legal start position.
    • Buzzer drill. Finish every practice run with a hard stop at 0:00, and practice releasing a bag just before it.
    • Referee Q&A role-play. Have students practice calmly asking the Head Referee a question after a Match.

    Events & rankings

    How a competition day is structured and how teams are ranked.

    Qualification Matches

    Teams are paired with randomly assigned partners. Local qualifiers run at least 6 Matches per team; Championship events run at least 8. Teams are ranked by average score.

    T8 · T9 · T10 · T12

    Finals

    Finals Alliances pair ranked teams in order (1st & 2nd, 3rd & 4th, …). Each Alliance plays one Match; the highest score wins. Events that send teams to Worlds need at least 5 Finals Matches in a division with 10+ teams.

    T12 · T13 · T14

    Robot Skills

    Each team gets exactly three Driving and three Autonomous Coding runs, first come first served. Your ranking score is your best Driving plus your best Autonomous run.

    T15 · T17

    No-show Matches are never excluded. Ties are broken by removing each team's lowest score and recomputing the average, repeating through all scores; any remaining tie is a random draw (T11). In leagues, teams that play under 60% of available Matches rank below teams that play more.

    Disqualifications at a glance

    • Qualification Match: the offending team scores zero. Its partner still keeps the Alliance points.
    • Finals Match: the whole Alliance scores zero.
    • Robot Skills: a DQ means a zero for that run.
    • No-show: zero for that team; the partner plays on and scores.
