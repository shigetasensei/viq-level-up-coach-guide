01 Volunteers

You don't need to know robots.

Most jobs at a robotics event are about being friendly, careful and on time. School staff, parents and high school students can all help. Pick a job, read the short list of tasks, and tell the lead you are in. Every job has a role sheet you can print.

No robot skills needed

Nine of the twelve jobs below need no robotics knowledge at all. The lead shows you what to do, and a short briefing starts your shift.

See students shine

Watch young people build, code and cheer each other on, and be part of a day your school community remembers. Ask your school whether volunteer hours count. Suggested

Short shifts

Most jobs are about two hours, so you can help for a part of the day and still enjoy it. Ask the lead for the shifts that fit you.

Where everything starts

A top view of the Field before a Teamwork Match: where the robots start, and where each Bean Bag, Goal and Load Zone sits. Each number matches the list below.

  1. Robot start, Team 1 (red) Beside the wall it shares with the blue Pyramid Goal. About 11″ wide and 20″ long, fully still.
  2. Robot start, Team 2 (blue) Beside the wall it shares with the red Pyramid Goal. Mirror image of Team 1.
  3. Yellow Preload Each robot starts with one yellow bag touching the robot and nothing else.
  4. Load Zones Red on the right wall near the top, blue on the left wall near the bottom. Loaders add bags here, one at a time.
  5. Floor Goals Red on the left wall, blue on the right wall. A bag must sit fully inside.
  6. Pyramid Goals Red in the top-left corner, blue in the bottom-right corner, each with levels L1, L2 and L3.
  7. L4 Goals Two tall platforms near the middle. Only yellow bags score here.
  8. Yellow walls Two walls from the long edges toward the middle that shape where robots can drive.
  9. Starting Bean Bags on the Field 8 red, 8 blue and 4 yellow on marked spots. The 4 yellow ones sit in the middle.
  10. Waiting Bean Bags 8 red beside the red Load Zone and 8 blue beside the blue Load Zone, for the Loaders. Total 38 with the 2 Preloads.

Pick a job

Check-in desk

Be the first friendly face teams see.

  • Find the team on the list and tick it off.
  • Hand out name tags and pit numbers.
  • Point the team to inspection and the pits.

Robotics: None No training 2 hours

Open role sheet

Guide and hospitality

Help visitors find their way and feel welcome.

  • Show people to restrooms, pits, Fields and lunch.
  • Keep walkways clear and doorways open.
  • Answer simple questions or find someone who can.

Robotics: None No training 2 hours

Open role sheet

Queuer

Make sure the right teams reach the right Field on time.

  • Call teams to the queue a few Matches ahead.
  • Check the Drive Team is ready: up to three students.
  • Tell the referee when both teams are ready.

Robotics: None Short briefing 2 hours

Open role sheet

Field reset crew

Put the Field back to the starting layout between Matches.

  • Wait until the referee says the Match is over.
  • Put Bean Bags and Goals back using the start-layout diagram.
  • Step away from the Field and give a thumbs up.

Robotics: None Short briefing 2 hours

Open role sheet

Timer and display helper

Run the clock and the screen so everyone can see the time.

  • Open the Match timer on the screen.
  • Press Start when the Head Referee says go.
  • Keep the screen showing the right Match.

Robotics: None Short briefing 2 hours

Open role sheet

Scoring helper

Help the scorekeeper record the Head Referee’s scores.

  • Write down the score the Head Referee reads out.
  • Read the number back to be sure.
  • Pass the sheet or screen to the scorekeeper.

Robotics: A little Training needed 2 hours

Open role sheet

Field referee helper

Watch one robot and tell the Head Referee what you see.

  • Stand at your Field and watch your robot or the Loader.
  • Tell the Head Referee if you see a problem.
  • Do not explain rulings to teams.

Robotics: A little Training needed 2 hours

Open role sheet

Inspection helper

Help the inspector check robots with a simple list.

  • Hold the sizing box and the checklist.
  • Check the two team number plates.
  • Fetch the inspector if you are not sure.

Robotics: A little Training needed 2 hours

Open role sheet

Runner and supplies

Be the person who gets things to where they are needed.

  • Carry messages between the lead, the desk and the Fields.
  • Bring water, spare batteries and tape.
  • Keep the supply table tidy.

Robotics: None No training 2 hours

Open role sheet

Photos and announcer helper

Share the day with photos, or read names and Match calls aloud.

  • Take photos only where the school allows.
  • Or read Match calls from a script.
  • Help the emcee find the next team.

Robotics: None Short briefing 2 hours

Open role sheet

Setup and pack-down crew

Make the day possible before and after the Matches.

  • Carry tables, set up pits and put up signs.
  • Help the lead assemble the Fields.
  • Pack everything away at the end.

Robotics: None Short briefing Morning or evening

Open role sheet

First-aid contact

Be the person everyone knows to call if someone is hurt.

  • Stay near the first-aid kit.
  • Look after people who feel unwell.
  • Call the school office or emergency help if needed.

Robotics: None School staff only All day

Open role sheet

For student volunteers

Suggested Work in pairs or near an adult lead. Sign in and out, take breaks, and never enter a Field unless a referee says so. Students competing on a team can still help at times when their team is not playing, if the Event Partner agrees.

For school staff

Suggested Staff can lead a station, mentor student volunteers, or help with first aid and safeguarding. Adults who are not event staff may not stand in the Driver Station during Matches. GG1

Rules for volunteers

Official Only the Head Referee explains rulings to teams. Referees must be approved by the Event Partner, and Head Referees must be 16 or older and certified. Scorekeeper Referees must be 15 or older. T1 · T2

V5 VEX V5 · Override

Volunteering at a V5 Override event.

Two of the upcoming competitions are VEX V5 Robotics Competitions, where the game is Override. The volunteer jobs are much the same as at a VEX IQ event, but the game is bigger and faster. Here is what to know, and a checklist for your day. Facts marked Official come from VEX's Override game page and the event pages; Suggested are this guide's ideas.

Stack Pins and Cups

Official Alliances stack alliance-colored Cups on Pins in Goals to score points.

Control the Toggles

Official Robots flip the Toggles on the field walls to their alliance color for points and control.

Finish in the Midfield

Official Robots park in the Midfield at the end of Driver Control for points.

A Match in two parts

0:15Autonomous
1:45Driver Control

Official Two Alliances, one red and one blue, with two teams each, play on a 12′ × 12′ square field. The Alliance with more points in Autonomous earns a 12-point bonus. Each Alliance can also earn an Autonomous Win Point by completing assigned tasks.

Override scoring values
WhatPointsNotes
Alliance-colored Pin in a Goal5Each Pin scored in a Goal.
Owned yellow Pin10A yellow Pin in a Goal is worth 10 for the Alliance that owns the Toggle in that Quadrant.
Robot in the Midfield8For ending the Match in the Midfield.
Autonomous Bonus12For the Alliance with more points in the Autonomous Period.

On the field

  • 56 Cups and 63 Pins.
  • 9 Goals: 4 neutral Short Goals, 1 neutral Tall Goal, 2 red and 2 blue Alliance Goals.
  • 4 Toggles, one at the center of each field wall.
  • Loaders next to each Alliance Station.

Official Check the field figures in the Game Manual before you reset.

How is it different from VEX IQ?

VEX IQ Level Up compared with VEX V5 Override
 VEX IQ · Level UpVEX V5 · Override
Field6′ × 8′12′ × 12′
Match60 seconds, driver controlled in Teamwork Matches15 s Autonomous, then 1:45 Driver Control
Who playsTwo teams, one robot each, as one AllianceTwo Alliances of two teams each, so four robots
Scoring objects38 Bean Bags63 Pins and 56 Cups
Event field systemNone listed on the event pageSmart Field Control, per the event pages

What your job looks like at V5

Suggested Use the twelve jobs above and their role sheets. These are the V5 differences to expect. Your lead will confirm the details.

V5 differences for common volunteer jobs
JobAt a V5 event
Check-in deskWelcome teams, hand out name tags and pit numbers, and send them to inspection. At MBIS, volunteer check-in runs 9:30 to 10:00.
QueuerEvery Match has four teams, two per Alliance. Call all four a few Matches ahead and check each robot is with its team.
Field reset crewMore to reset: Pins, Cups, nine Goals and four Toggles. Resets take longer, so work in pairs and use the Game Manual's field figures.
Timer and displayAsk your lead how the Match timer and field control are run at your event. Never start or stop a Match yourself unless the Head Referee tells you to.
Scoring helperLearn the four score values above. Only the Head Referee decides what scores, and the scorekeeper records it.
Referee helperTraining is needed. Watch where you are told and report to the Head Referee. Do not explain rulings to teams.
Inspection helperWork from the inspection checklist next to the inspector, who makes the decisions.
Runner, guide, setup, first aidSame as the jobs above. Bigger fields and more teams mean more walking, so wear comfortable shoes.
