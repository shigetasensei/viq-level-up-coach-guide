Referee zone for volunteers

Referee zone: a guide for volunteer referees at VEX IQ Level Up eventsCalm eyes. Fair calls.

Helping at a VEX IQ event as a referee? Here is what you need on the day: the game in two minutes, how a Match runs, what to watch for, how the score is read, and what to say when a team has a question.

01 Your role

You keep the game fair.

Referees do not coach, cheer or help a team win. You make sure every team plays by the same rules, and you make it a good day for the students. Facts marked Official come from the Game Manual; ideas marked Suggested are this guide's author's.

Head Referee

Official Has the last word on gameplay and robot rulings, and is the only person who explains rules, disqualifications or violations to teams. Must be 16 or older, approved by the Event Partner and certified for this season.

T1T2

Scorekeeper Referee

Official Records scores for the Head Referee. Must be 15 or older. Scorekeepers do not explain rulings to teams.

T2GG14

Field volunteers

Suggested Queuing teams, resetting the Field, running the timer and watching one robot each. Your Head Referee or Event Partner decides who does what, so ask before the first Match.

T4

Don't see a violation? Say nothing.

Suggested Watch, don't hunt. Rules give the benefit of the doubt for accidents and for things that are too close to call.

G3GG12

The Head Referee decides

Official If you see something, tell the Head Referee. Do not rule on it, argue it, or explain it to the team yourself.

T1

Not your own team

Suggested If you coach, teach or are related to a team, ask not to referee or score its Matches. It keeps the call clean for everyone, including you.

Certification and training are set by the organization that runs the event. Ask your Event Partner which course you need, and read the Game Manual's referee notes before the event. This page is a quick guide, not a replacement for either.

02 The game

Level Up in two minutes.

Two robots on one Alliance work together for 60 seconds, moving Bean Bags into Goals. Higher Goals are worth more.

One bag at a time

A robot may Possess or Plow only one Bean Bag at a time. This is the rule you will watch most. Brief, incidental contact that barely moves a bag is not Plowing.

SG6

Drive Team of three

Each team has up to three students in the Driver Station: Driver 1, Driver 2 and a Loader. No adults other than event staff, and no communication devices in use.

GG1

The hand-off

Drivers hand over the controller between 0:35 and 0:25 remaining. No Driver may drive more than 35 seconds.

GG11

03 A Match, step by step

Same routine, every Match.

A steady routine keeps the event on time and treats every team the same. Your Head Referee may change the details, so follow their lead.

  1. 01

    Check the Field.

    Before teams arrive: nothing is broken or loose, and Bean Bags and Goals are in their starting places. Field parts can vary by about ½″ between Fields, which is normal. Tell the Head Referee about anything that could change play. T5GG4

  2. 02

    Teams arrive.

    The robot must be in the Driver Station or on the Field for the team's Match, even if it is broken. A team that doesn't show scores 0, but its partner still plays and scores. Only the Drive Team (up to three students) stands in the Driver Station. GG1GG2

  3. 03

    Set the robots.

    Each robot sits in a legal start position, touching only the Floor, the Field Perimeter and its one yellow Preload, and is fully still. Team 1 / red starts beside the blue Pyramid Goal; Team 2 / blue beside the red one. Don't hurry the team, but don't let setup drag on. SG1SG5GG3

  4. 04

    Start.

    Check both Drive Teams are ready, then start the timer. Nobody may drive or move a robot before the timer starts. GG12

  5. 05

    Watch.

    Keep your eyes on your robots and on the Loaders, not the score. Use the watch list below. Note anything you plan to tell the Head Referee. GG4SG6SG7

  6. 06

    Buzzer at 0:00.

    Everything stops. A bag released before 0:00 finishes its path and counts once it comes to rest. Motion after 0:00 never scores. If it is too close to call, the team gets the benefit of the doubt. GG12

  7. 07

    Wait for everything to rest.

    Do not touch any bag or robot until everything has stopped. If you must move an object to judge another, agree with all teams and the Head Referee what the first one's status was. SC1

  8. 08

    Score it.

    The Head Referee judges the score by eye. No photos or video. Read it aloud, then record it. SC2

  9. 09

    Questions.

    Student Drive Team Members who stay in the Driver Station may talk to the Head Referee about a ruling. Only the Head Referee explains it, and once it is final, it is final. GG14T3

  10. 10

    Reset and thank.

    Reset the Field to the starting layout, make sure teams collect their robots, and say thank you. A smile helps more than you think.

04 Watch list

What to look for.

These are the situations that come up most. When you see one, tell the Head Referee. The Head Referee decides what it is and what happens next. The wording below is a plain-language summary, so check the linked rule if you are unsure.

Common situations, what the rule says and what to do
You see…The rule saysYouRule
A robot holding or pushing two bagsOnly one bag may be Possessed or Plowed. The robot must immediately stop everything except trying to shed the extra bag. A light touch that barely moves a bag isn't Plowing.Note it and tell the Head Referee.SG6
The Loader adds a bagOne at a time. Red and blue bags only in the matching-color Load Zone. The bag touches the Floor, sits fully inside the Zone, and no other bag touches that Zone. Never placed directly onto a robot, and a robot may not touch a bag a human is touching.Watch the Loader and the Zone together.SG7
A hand or foot goes into the FieldNo one may touch Field Elements, robots or objects in play, or reach into the Field, except for permitted resets and loading.Tell the Head Referee. Don't step in.GG4
An adult in the Driver Station, or a coach shoutingOnly the Drive Team stands in the Driver Station, and adults (other than event staff) may not. People outside may cheer but not direct. No communication devices in use.Tell the Head Referee.GG1
The controller changes hands too early, too late or neverThe hand-off happens between 0:35 and 0:25 remaining. No Driver drives more than 35 seconds, and the first Driver can't touch the controls afterward.Watch the clock at 0:35 and 0:25.GG11

Something is damaged or a part falls offDamage that needs repair before the next Match is usually a Minor Violation. Dropped parts are no longer part of the robot.Tell the Head Referee. Pause the next Match if the Field needs repair.GG9GG8
Anything unsafeUnsafe operation can get a team Disabled or Disqualified. Students must never step onto the Field.Stay safe, tell the Head Referee at once.S1

Disqualification and violation levels (Minor, Major, Score Affecting) are the Head Referee's call. In a Qualification Match a DQ zeroes that team only, and in Finals it zeroes the whole Alliance. GG6. Read the Game Manual glossary for the full definitions.

05 Reading the score

Score only what has stopped.

When the buzzer sounds and everything is at rest, go Goal by Goal. For each bag, ask the same short questions.

Floor Goal · 1 point

  • Not touching a robot.
  • Entirely inside the Goal, nothing overhanging.
  • Yellow, or the Goal's own color.

SC3

L1 · L2 · L3 · 3 / 6 / 12

  • Not touching a robot.
  • Touches that level's flat surface, or is held up by a bag that does.
  • Not touching anything outside the Field.
  • Yellow, or the Pyramid's color.
  • A bag that qualifies on two levels scores only the higher one.

SC4

L4 · 16 points

  • Not touching a robot.
  • On the L4 surface, or held up by a bag that is.
  • Yellow only. Red and blue never score on L4.

SC5

Touching a robot?

A bag a robot is still touching does not score anywhere. If the robot can't release it without power, that is a robot rule. Note it for the Head Referee. R5

Count out loud

Suggested Say each Goal's count as you go, such as “Red Floor: 4”, so the scorekeeper and the Drive Teams can follow and catch a miscount before it is recorded.

Practice first

Suggested The 3D lab has a quiz mode where you call the score before the explanation appears. Ten minutes there before the event is time well spent.

06 Tough moments

When a team disagrees.

Most disagreements are about a score or a violation. The students are often tired, nervous and excited. Slow down, stay kind and follow the process.

Who may ask?

Official Only the student Drive Team Members from that Match, by staying in the Driver Station. Not coaches, parents or other students. Send an adult who comes to you to the Head Referee.

GG14SC2

Who answers?

Official Only the Head Referee explains a ruling. The Head Referee's decision is final, and no photos or video are reviewed. If you are not the Head Referee, say so kindly and bring them over.

T1T3

What about replays?

Official Replays are rare. The Event Partner and Head Referee must agree, and usually only for a Field fault that affected the score. There are no time-outs.

GG5GG7

Say it like this

  • “Thanks for waiting. The Head Referee will talk with you now.”
  • “Tell us what you saw.” (Listen, then stop talking.)
  • “I can't change the ruling, but I'll make sure the Head Referee hears you.”

Avoid

  • Guessing what the rule is. Check the manual.
  • Hinting at how to win or how to fix a robot.
  • Discussing a ruling with coaches or other teams.
  • Raising your voice back.

If it gets heated

Step back, and call the Head Referee or the Event Partner. Respectful behavior is part of the Code of Conduct, and serious problems are handled case by case. Never argue it out at the Field.

G1T4

07 Robot Skills

If you referee Skills.

A Skills Match is one robot alone on a Skills Field. All the rules above still apply unless this section changes them. A violation in a Skills Match affects only that Match.

What is different in Robot Skills Matches
TopicWhat is differentRule
SetupEight Bean Bags (4 red, 4 blue) start in the red Driver Station for loading. The blue Driver Station and Load Zone aren't used. The robot starts beside the red Pyramid Goal, at the start and after any reset. The layout differs from Teamwork; check Figure RSC3-1 in the manual.RSC3
LoadingEverything loads through the red Load Zone, with the same one-at-a-time rules. Any Driver who isn't driving can load. In Driving Skills the team stays in the red Driver Station.RSC4
Autonomous startThe run starts with a button on the Brain or by manually triggering a sensor. Starting with the controller is not allowed.RSC6
Autonomous runOnce it starts, no one may use a controller or trigger a sensor, even without touching it, including the Vision Sensor. A controller at the Field stays on the Floor, untouched.RSC7
Handling the robotIn Autonomous Coding Skills the team may pick up the robot as often as it likes. Each time it goes straight back to a legal start position, with no new Preload, and any bag it held is removed.RSC5
Stop TimeA team may opt in before the run to end it early. Agree the signal with the scorekeeper first (for example, “Stop!” with crossed arms) and reconfirm before every run. It is only a ranking tiebreaker.RSC8
