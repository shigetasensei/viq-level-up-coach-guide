Parents family support

Cheer loud, hands off the robot: how parents can help a VEX IQ team at home and at competitionsCheer loud. Hands off.

Your child's team designs, builds, programs and drives the robot. You make the season possible: rides, rest, snacks, patience, and the best cheering in the room. This page shows what helps at home, what helps at a competition, and where the rules ask adults to step back.

01 Your role

Be the support crew, not the build crew.

VEX IQ is a student-centered program. The rules say students do the designing, building, programming and strategy, and adults teach, ask questions and encourage. That is good news: you do not need to know anything about robots to be a great robotics parent.

Students do the work

Students design, build, program and plan. If a robot built or programmed by an adult scores points, the violation is automatically Score Affecting. Official G2

Adults teach and encourage

Coaches may teach ideas and ask questions. Parents can do the same at home: listen, ask “what did you try?” and let the answer be theirs. Official G2

Everyone follows the Code of Conduct

All attendees, parents and guests included, must follow the Code of Conduct. Be kind to every team, referee and volunteer. Official G1

✓ Parents can

  • Ask open questions: What are you building? What went wrong? What will you try next?
  • Drive, feed and rest the team. Plan rides, food, water and sleep.
  • Keep the calendar: practices, event dates, forms and fees.
  • Watch a practice run and say what you noticed, as an audience.
  • Cheer positively, for every team.
  • Volunteer at the event, in a job away from your own child's Matches.

✕ Parents shouldn't

  • Build, fix, design or program the robot, or write any of the engineering notebook.
  • Plan the team's strategy or choose the drivers.
  • Enter the Driver Station, or direct the Drive Team during a Match.
  • Speak to the Head Referee about a score or a call. Only the student Drive Team may.
  • Answer a judge's question for a student.
  • Be upset at the team, a partner team or the referees in public.

Official The full wording is in the Student-Centered Policy ↗ and in the Game Manual. The lists above are this guide's short version of it.

02 At home

Small things that make a big difference.

Most of what helps happens outside practice. Suggested

Learn the game together

Ask your child to teach you Level Up. Two robots move Bean Bags into Goals during a 60-second Match, and higher Goals score more. Explaining it is great practice for a judge interview.

5-minute briefing → Match timer →

Ask, don't solve

When your child is stuck, try one of these. “What did you expect to happen?” “What actually happened?” “What is one thing you could change and test?” Then stop talking and let them think.

Let failing be part of it

Robots break, code has bugs and Matches go wrong. A team that tests, finds the problem and tries again is doing exactly what robotics teaches. Praise the effort and the thinking, not only the score.

Read the notebook, don't write it

The engineering notebook is a record of the students' own thinking, and it is judged. Ask to see it and tell them what you understood. An adult must never write any part of it. Official G2

Protect sleep, food and homework

Tired teams make mistakes. Help your child balance robotics with school and rest, especially in the week before an event, and help them plan time instead of cramming.

Be the logistics person

Keep track of dates and forms, ask your child's coach what they need, and plan the ride. Every student needs a participant release form on file, so watch for the coach's message. Official S3

Questions for the dinner table

  • What was the hardest problem your team solved this week?
  • What did you try that did not work?
  • What is your job on the team, and who helps you with it?
  • How did your team decide what to do next?
  • What would you do differently in the next Match?
  • Who on your team helped you today?

Home practice that fits

  • If the coach shares it, try Virtual Driving Skills together. Taking turns is half the fun.
  • Time a 60-second job around the house, such as sorting laundry, with the Match timer.
  • Let your child present the robot to the family as practice for the judges.
  • Ask them to explain a rule from the rules explorer in their own words.

Suggested

Think big, spend small

You do not need to buy parts or build a practice field at home. Ask the coach what the team actually needs before you buy anything.

03 At the competition

A long day. Here is how to be a help.

A competition has many short Matches, long waits and a lot of noise. Students work hard to stay calm. Be the calm adult. Suggested

Watching a Match

New to the game? Each Match is 60 seconds. Look for the Bean Bags landing in Goals and, at about the halfway mark, a hand-off where the second Driver takes the controller. Watch the Match walk-through to know what you are seeing.

Photos and videos

Photos are a great way to remember the day. Stay behind the Field boundary, keep walkways clear, and ask the host about any event rules for filming. Do not post photos of other people's children without asking. Match video is not used to review calls. Official

When something goes wrong

If your child is upset or the robot breaks, offer a hug and a snack, then let the team decide what to do. If you are worried about safety or behavior, tell the coach or the event host, who can take it to the Event Partner. Please do not take it to a referee in the middle of a Match.

04 Volunteer

Events run on parents who say yes.

Every competition needs a lot of hands, and most jobs need no robot knowledge. Volunteering is also the best seat in the house.

Teams bring volunteers

On our event pages, each team must provide at least one volunteer. Volunteers may be coaches, parents, family, friends, alumni or other supporters. Official Ask your coach how your team is covering it.

Dates and sign-up →

Easy jobs to start with

Check-in desk, guide and hospitality, queuer, scoring helper, timer and display helper, runner and supplies, photos, setup and pack-down. The lead gives a short briefing, and each job has a printable role sheet. Suggested

See the twelve jobs →

A few tips

  • Pick a shift when your child's team is not playing.
  • Ask the lead which jobs suit you. Roles that score, referee or judge can be a poor fit if your own child's team is playing.
  • Wear comfortable shoes and bring water.
  • Be the example: kind, calm and on time.

Suggested

Want to referee or judge?

Referees and judges follow their own guides. Read the Referee zone or the Judge guide, then ask the Event Partner whether they need help. Suggested

06 Questions

Questions parents ask.

Do I need to know robotics or coding?

No. Curiosity and encouragement are enough. Most volunteer jobs need no robotics knowledge, and your child can explain the rest. Suggested

The robot is broken. Can I fix it?

No. Students build, repair and program their robot, and an adult who does so can cause a Score Affecting violation. Offer a snack, ask what they think went wrong, and let them fix it. Official G2

Can I stand with the team at the Field?

You may watch from outside the Field. Only the Drive Team (up to three students) may be in the Driver Station, and adults other than event staff may not. Please do not call out directions during a Match. Official GG1

I disagree with a call. What can I do?

Stay calm and stay back. Only the student Drive Team may discuss a ruling with the Head Referee, right after the Match. If you have a concern about conduct, tell the coach. Official GG14

Can I volunteer even though my child competes?

Yes. Our event pages say volunteers may be coaches, parents, family, friends, alumni or other supporters, and each team must provide at least one. Choose a shift when your child's team is not playing. Official Sign-up links

Can I record my child's Match?

Usually yes, from outside the Field, but check with the host first, and keep walkways clear. Video is not used to review calls. Do not post other people's children without asking. Suggested

How long is the day?

Plan for most of a day. Each Match is 60 seconds, but there are waits between Matches, plus inspection, judging and awards. Ask your coach for the schedule; the schedule planner shows a sample. Suggested

Where can I read the official information?

In the Student-Centered Policy and the Game Manual. The manual's plain-language Simplified Edition ↗ is written for students and the adults who support them. Official
