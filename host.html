Host zone for organizers

Host zone: a guide for the school and Event Partner hosting a VEX IQ Level Up competitionYour school. Your event.

Hosting a VEX IQ competition at your school? This page is for the Event Partner and the school staff who make the day happen: who does what, what the rules require of a host, what to borrow or buy, and a to-do list that counts down to your event date.

Check the current hosting rules first

Who administers VEX IQ competition changed in 2026. VEX Robotics told Event Partners on May 10, 2026 that its competitions are now run by VEX in coordination with the Global Robotics & Science Foundation, and that existing event authorizations and access to VEX systems stay in place. Reports about this change differ, and the official Event Partner pages were not all public when this page was written. Confirm requirements, fees, training and software with events.vex.com and your VEX contact. Anything marked Check needs that confirmation.

01 Who does what

A team of adults, and a lot of helpers.

An Event Partner leads the planning team, and the school provides the venue and people. Facts marked Official come from the Game Manual; Suggested are this guide's ideas for a school event with about 20 to 30 teams, two Teamwork Fields and one or two Skills Fields.

03 What the rules say

What the Game Manual asks of a host.

These are the Tournament rules that most affect your planning. The wording is a plain-language summary, so read the linked rule for the full text.

Fields

Official Field Elements and object positions can vary by about ½″. Aesthetic repairs are allowed, but changes that affect gameplay are not. Teamwork Fields at one event must carry the same permitted changes.

T5 T6 T7

Qualification Matches

Official Every team plays at least 6 Qualification Matches at a local event, with Alliance partners set randomly by the software. The published schedule is followed, and a Match may start early only if everyone is ready.

T8 T9 T12

Ranking

Official Teams are ranked by average Qualification score after dropping the lowest scores, so a team with 4 to 7 Matches drops one. Ties are broken by the rules and the software.

T10 T11

Finals

Official You set the number of Finals Matches. Events that qualify teams for the World Championship need at least five per division when ten or more teams attend. Each Alliance plays one Finals Match, lowest ranked first.

T13 T14

Skills

Official Skills runs are first come, first served, with three Driving and three Autonomous Coding runs per team. Only teams that play Qualification Matches may run Skills. Skills Fields may differ from Teamwork Fields, but teams must be told.

T15 T16 T17

Replays, appeals, authority

Official The Head Referee has the last word on gameplay, and appeals come only from student Drive Team Members. Replays are rare and need the Event Partner and Head Referee to agree. There are no time-outs.

T1 T3 GG5 GG7

What else a host must do (registration, fees, insurance, software, award rules and reporting results) comes from VEX and your regional contact, not from the Game Manual. Check each with them.

      06 Equipment

      What to have on the day.

      The Level Up Field is 6′ × 8′ and uses 38 Bean Bags: 16 red, 16 blue and 6 yellow. Plan floor space for every Field plus room to walk around it.

      For each Field

      • A complete Level Up Field set, assembled, level and in repair.
      • 38 Bean Bags, plus a way to mend them. T6
      • A Match timer, or the Match timer on a big screen.
      • Field labels, and a start-position diagram for resets.

      For the event

      • A laptop and display for Tournament Manager, a backup laptop, and a speaker for announcements.
      • Extension cords, power strips and a Wi-Fi plan.
      • Pit tables, chairs, team number signs and name tags.
      • An inspection station with a sizing box (11″ × 20″ × 15″). R3
      • Printed referee checklists and backup score sheets.

      Small things that save the day

      • Tape, cable ties, scissors, markers and a tape measure.
      • Spare batteries and chargers. Some teams will forget theirs.
      • A first-aid kit, water and a quiet space.
      • A sewing kit for torn Bean Bags.

      Ask VEX or your regional contact which Field products and software are currently authorized. Check Prices and availability change.

      08 School matters

      Things only a school can plan.

      These are Suggested ideas. Your school's own policies come first.

      Safety and supervision

      Every student must be supervised by a responsible adult the whole time, so tell visiting coaches in advance. Have a first-aid contact, clear exits and a lost-child plan. Students may never step onto the Field.

      S1 S2

      Families and visitors

      Decide who may enter, how visitors sign in and where spectators stand. Agree a photo and video policy with the school and make it clear to families. Scoring is judged by eye, never from video.

      SC2

      Access and inclusion

      Check step-free routes, quiet spaces, restrooms and seating. If teams speak different languages, ask volunteers who can help and put up simple printed signs.

      Your students as helpers

      Students can queue teams, reset Fields, run the timer and referee with an adult nearby. Make sure they know the role before the day, and give them breaks too.

      Referee zone →

      Food, power and parking

      Plan lunch (including allergies), enough power for laptops and displays, and where buses and cars can park and unload robots and tables.

      Conduct

      All attendees follow the Code of Conduct. Have the Event Partner or a named adult ready to handle problems away from the Field, so referees can focus on Matches.

      G1 T4

      Level Up.

      Plan early. Brief everyone. Thank them twice.
